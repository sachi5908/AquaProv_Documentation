Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

From laboratory concentrations to chemical equivalents

A mass concentration gives the mass of an ion; an equivalent concentration also accounts for its electric charge. Let Cᵢ be concentration in mg/L, Mᵢ molar mass in g/mol (numerically also mg/mmol), and |zᵢ| the absolute ionic charge (Hem, 1985):

mᵢ = Cᵢ / Mᵢ (mmol/L)
eᵢ = |zᵢ| Cᵢ / Mᵢ (meq/L)

For an input already in meq/L, eᵢ is the supplied value. To recover mmol/L from meq/L, divide by |zᵢ|. These conversions are different for divalent ions such as Ca²⁺ and monovalent ions such as Na⁺.

Ion Mᵢ (g/mol) Absolute charge
Ca 40.0780 2
Mg 24.3050 2
Na 22.9898 1
K 39.0983 1
HCO₃ 61.0171 1
CO₃ 60.0092 2
Cl 35.4527 1
SO₄ 96.0636 2

Illustrative check: 40.078 mg/L Ca is 1 mmol/L and 2 meq/L; 22.9898 mg/L Na is 1 mmol/L and 1 meq/L. Equal molar concentrations do not mean equal charge contributions. Concentrations must be finite and nonnegative. A missing measurement does not become a measured zero; check the selected missing-data policy and any explicitly supplied values.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/ions.py (ions_WEIGHT, ions_CHARGE), aquaprovpy/_chemistry.py (major_ion_meq).

Normalize cations and anions separately

Using equivalents eᵢ, form the two charge totals and the six grouped fractions:

Tc = eCa + eMg + eNa + eK
Ta = eHCO₃ + eCO₃ + eCl + eSO₄
c = (eCa, eMg, eNa + eK) / Tc
a = (eHCO₃ + eCO₃, eSO₄, eCl) / Ta

Below, c₁, c₂, c₃ mean Ca, Mg and Na+K fractions, and a₁, a₂, a₃ mean HCO₃+CO₃, SO₄ and Cl fractions. Each triplet sums to 1; multiply by 100 for equivalent percentages. Both totals must be positive. AquaProv checks this before constructing normalized major-ion diagrams.

Normalization explains why these diagrams describe relative composition. Multiplying every concentration in a sample by ten leaves its position unchanged. Separate normalization also hides disagreement between cation and anion totals, so a plausible plotted position does not establish good charge balance (Piper, 1944).

Implementation: aquaprovpy/_chemistry.py and the normalization in each major-ion diagram's plot function.

Piecewise HFE-D coordinates and reference mixing line

The hydrochemical facies evolution diagram was developed to examine salinization and freshening around the seawater interface (Giménez-Forcada, 2010). Let C = 100c, A = 100a, rc = max(C₁, C₂), and ra = max(A₁, A₂). The implementation switches branches according to the dominant competing group:

x = rc − 100/3 + 66.7 if rc > C₃; otherwise x = 100 − C₃
y = ra − 100/3 + 66.7 if ra > A₃; otherwise y = 100 − A₃

Here C₃ is Na+K%, A₃ is Cl%, and A₁ combines HCO₃+CO₃. The 66.7 constant is the drawing convention used in the source. These piecewise coordinates arrange the reference facies used to discuss salinization and freshening.

The reference line joins a fixed seawater composition to (max x, max y) from the plotted dataset. Those two maxima need not belong to the same observed sample. The seawater endpoint uses the bundled Ca, Mg, Na, K, HCO₃, Cl and SO₄ concentrations converted to equivalents. Consequently the line is a visual reference, not an estimated seawater mixing fraction or independently measured recharge end member. Temporal observations are needed to infer a direction of evolution.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/hfed.py (plot, CONC_SWAWATER).

How it works#

The Hydrochemical Facies Evolution Diagram groups and transforms major-ion equivalent percentages to compare freshening and salinization pathways against its reference framework.

Use the plotted ion-group percentages and the method-specific facies fields.

Reference: Giménez-Forcada (2010).

Interpretation limits#

Keep the measurement context

A single plotted sample does not establish a direction of change. The figure does not simulate seawater transport, date intrusion, or infer a unique reaction path.
