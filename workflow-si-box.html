Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

Thermodynamic definition of mineral saturation index

For the mineral dissolution reaction written in the selected PHREEQC database, species activities determine the ion activity product (IAP). Let νⱼ be reaction stoichiometric exponents and K(T) its equilibrium constant at the solution temperature (Parkhurst & Appelo, 2013):

IAP = ∏ⱼaⱼνⱼ
SI = log₁₀[IAP/K(T)] = ∑ⱼνⱼlog₁₀aⱼ − log₁₀K(T)
aⱼ = γⱼmⱼ (on the database's activity standard-state basis)

Here mⱼ is species molality and γⱼ the activity coefficient; water activity enters where required by the database reaction. For calcite dissolution CaCO₃(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻, IAP = aCa²⁺aCO₃²⁻. Total calcium and total inorganic carbon are not automatically the free-species activities. For dolomite, the analogous product is aCa²⁺aMg²⁺aCO₃²⁻². PHREEQC uses each phase's actual database reaction; AquaProv does not hard-code an approximate concentration product.

Illustrative check: IAP/K = 0.1 gives SI = −1; a ratio of 1 gives SI = 0; a ratio of 10 gives SI = +1. One SI unit is a tenfold change in the saturation ratio. Negative SI permits dissolution if the mineral is present and accessible; positive SI indicates thermodynamic precipitation potential, which may be inhibited by kinetics or nucleation. SI is not mineral abundance or a reaction rate.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/backend.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/engine.py.

Background calculation: aqueous speciation and activities

In calculation mode AquaProv writes the mapped elemental totals, measured pH and solution conditions to IPhreeqc. PHREEQC solves aqueous mass-action and component-balance equations under those inputs; the selected database controls complexes, equilibrium constants and the activity model. Ionic strength, I = ½∑ⱼmⱼzⱼ², links the full dissolved composition to activity coefficients. Thus omitting major ions can affect the SI of a mineral even when its own components are measured (Charlton & Parkhurst, 2011; Parkhurst & Appelo, 2013).

pH constrains hydrogen activity by pH = −log₁₀aH⁺. Alkalinity or DIC constrains carbonate chemistry; bicarbonate concentration is not silently treated as a titrated alkalinity. Density relates volume-based inputs to solvent-mass concentrations. Temperature changes equilibrium constants and species distributions. For an explicitly supplied Eh versus the standard hydrogen electrode, pe = F·Eh/(ln(10)RT), with Eh in volts and T in kelvin. Missing redox information cannot be inferred merely from a total-iron concentration.

The workflow speciates initial solutions at 1 atm. It neither equilibrates the water with selected minerals nor adjusts an ion to force charge balance. The worked dataset uses phreeqc.dat, 25 °C, density 1 kg/L and TA as CaCO₃; these are stated assumptions, not all measured properties. Review Mineral SI inputs and reporting bases before changing databases or using saline/redox-sensitive water.

In import mode, the supplied SI values are preserved without repeating this calculation. A database/version, source rounding and activity model cannot be recovered from the SI column alone. Undefined PHREEQC sentinels −999.999 and −999 become unassessed, not extreme undersaturation.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/backend.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/automatic.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/data.py (import_si).

Exact classes and mineral-specific sample counts

A finite SI < 0 is Undersaturated; SI = 0 is At equilibrium; SI > 0 is Supersaturated. The classifier uses full numerical values with no hidden tolerance or rounding. A label rounded to 0.00 may therefore still be negative or positive. A zero in imported data may also reflect rounding by its source.

Per-mineral summaries use finite SI values to calculate mean, median, sample SD (denominator n−1), quartiles and extremes. Class percentage = 100 × class count / that mineral's assessed count. Missing SI is excluded from this denominator and reported separately. For 8 assessed values with 6 supersaturated and 2 undersaturated, supersaturation is 75%, even if two other source records were unassessed. Comparing minerals requires checking their potentially different assessed counts.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/data.py (classify, summarize).

Quartiles, whiskers and individual extremes

For a set of finite plotted values v, let Q₁, Q₂ and Q₃ be the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles. AquaProv's NumPy/Matplotlib path uses linear quantiles. If sorted values are v₍₀₎…v₍ₙ₋₁₎, a quantile q lies at index (n−1)q and linearly interpolates neighbouring values (Hyndman & Fan, 1996).

IQR = Q₃ − Q₁
Lower fence = Q₁ − 1.5 IQR;   upper fence = Q₃ + 1.5 IQR

The box spans Q₁ to Q₃ with a median line. Whiskers reach the most extreme observations within the fences, not necessarily the fence coordinates. Values beyond the whiskers remain observations; the plot does not remove them. For values 1, 2, 3, 4, 9, Q₁ = 2, median = 3, Q₃ = 4, and the upper fence is 7, so 9 is beyond the whisker. This descriptive range is not a confidence interval (Tukey, 1977).

SI boxes with all observed points

Each mineral's box uses its finite, unstandardized SI values. Separate boxplot fliers are hidden because AquaProv overlays all observations, coloured by their exact saturation class. Horizontal jitter separates overlapping markers and does not alter SI. The box follows the usual 1.5-IQR rule; the points retain extremes beyond it. A median SI above zero describes the retained observations and is not a confidence statement about the entire aquifer.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/plots.py (view='box').

The worked figure#

Source data & selection#

All 47 source samples retained in the audit. PHREEQC calculation with phreeqc.dat: Calcite, Dolomite, Gypsum, Anhydrite, Halite and Fluorite. Incomplete chemistry at samples 30 and 31 is excluded from speciation; zero sulfate or fluoride activity can leave additional mineral results undefined (Sahoo et al., 2025).

Automatic chemistry mapping; temperature 25 °C, density 1 kg/L, TA as CaCO3. No measured redox constraint. These are documented demonstration assumptions, not independently reviewed laboratory conditions.

Reproduce it in AquaProv

  1. Open Mineral SI and select SI box plot in the left Plot library. Use its search box to find the plot.
  2. Use Load data in the top toolbar, Ctrl + I, or Data → Dataset → Import CSV or Excel. Import the exact input linked on this page. Select the intended worksheet for a multi-sheet workbook and check the row count and headers in the Data inspector.
  3. Turn Auto update off while entering the worked choices. In the right inspector, open the named tab and click a section heading to expand it; scroll down for the lower controls.
  4. Open Data → Mineral saturation data → Source. Keep Automatic: detect SI or water chemistry for the Sukinda chemistry example; it uses recognized SI columns when present and otherwise calculates with PHREEQC. Choose the sample/event identifier.
  5. Open Data → PHREEQC solution settings. The worked calculation uses phreeqc.dat, the measured pH column, constant temperature 25 °C and density 1 kg/L. Use Map dissolved chemistry and units… to review ion mappings and TA as CaCO3. Leave unmeasured redox unconstrained and review the incomplete-chemistry exclusion policy.
  6. For a manual calculation, choose Source → Calculate with PHREEQC and confirm I reviewed units, chemical basis and solution conditions. For imported SI, choose Import PHREEQC saturation indices and review mineral/column mappings and database provenance.
  7. Open Data → Minerals, samples and groups to select Calcite, Dolomite, Gypsum, Anhydrite, Halite and Fluorite. Use Compare samples… for sample IDs 1–12 in the bar example; retain all selected samples in the other examples. Review the Group / season column and Show group / season filter.
  8. Open Settings → SI figure settings for the mineral(s) and view-specific display. The mineral scatter uses Dolomite on X and Calcite on Y; maps show Calcite. A fixed symmetric SI colour scale helps compare figures; missing SI stays unassessed.
  9. Open Settings → Distribution and point appearance for distribution fill/outline and the individual observation overlay. Preserve observed points when reviewing spread; sparse groups may not support a violin density.
  10. Click Generate figure or Generate map, or press F5. Open Export → Results & methods to review exclusions, diagnostics, numerical tables and methods before interpreting or exporting the figure.

Read the figure#

Read SI relative to zero: negative is undersaturated, positive is supersaturated, and an exact zero is equilibrium. Saturation does not establish mineral abundance or precipitation rate. Unassessed cells are not zero. All figures use the selected database and stated solution conditions.

Refine the figure style

  1. In the right inspector, open Settings → Plot settings. Use Show plot title and Manual plot title (optional); leave the title blank for the automatic title. Set title size, weight, style and position here.
  2. Open Style → Publication figure → Typography. Choose the font family and annotation, axis-label, tick and legend sizes. The top toolbar also provides quick typography controls.
  3. In Style → Publication figure, open Labels & ticks to edit axis labels, visibility, limits, ticks and tick formats. Enter the colourbar title in Color scale label (blank = original). Keep quantity and units in labels; blank labels and limits retain the original labels and automatic bounds.
  4. In the same Publication figure panel, open Canvas & grid to set backgrounds, axis borders, grid visibility and layout padding. For geographic frames, use the map-specific Settings → Coordinates and grid controls when available.
  5. Open Style → Publication figure → Legend & color scale. Set Legend position (also draggable), columns, frame and individual Legend items for this plot. Choose Colour scale, Show colourbar and colourbar text sizes for numeric colours. Sample plots need a numeric colour column for a continuous scale; categorical groups use their group colours. Normalize colour scale to 0–1 changes display labels while retaining measurements and scientific classifications.
  6. Open Style → Publication figure → Dimensions & export. Choose Figure sizing → Custom, then set Width (inches) and Height (inches). Review Transparent background and Crop to content; cropping can change exported dimensions.
  7. Click Apply style and inspect the figure at its intended final size. Save preset and Load preset reuse presentation settings. Method, variable, grouping, sample-symbol and interpolation changes require Generate when Auto update is off.

Export and preserve

  1. Finish the calculation with Generate figure or Generate map (or F5). Read the warnings, then apply the final style.
  2. Open Export → Figure export. Enter File name, use Save to → Browse to choose your output folder, then select File format and Image resolution (DPI). Use distinct names for alternative results.
  3. Click the format-specific button, such as Export PNG, Export SVG or Export PDF. Open the saved file and check labels, legends, dimensions and the current view. Raster pixel dimensions depend on figure size and DPI.
  4. Open Export → Results & methods. Select a result table to inspect it. Choose Report format: CSV + JSON, Excel (.xlsx), Word (.docx) or PDF (.pdf), then click its export button. Available tables depend on the plot; some geochemical diagrams do not expose a table for every graphical element.
  5. Press Ctrl + S to save the .aquaprov project, including its data and per-plot settings. Keep the original measurements, exported results and final figure together.
