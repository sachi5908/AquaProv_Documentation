Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

What a sample-location map calculates

Each retained coordinate pair maps to a marker in the selected display CRS. A background map can reproject the display to Web Mercator; that changes graphical coordinates, not a well's measurement. A displayed attribute can colour a marker, but no value is estimated between markers. A location map has no interpolation equation, prediction variance or groundwater-flow solution. Coordinate metadata, datum, longitude/latitude order and source units determine whether the points are positioned correctly (Snyder, 1987).

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/maps/workspace.py, aquaprovpy/locations.py, aquaprovpy/boundaries.py.

Coordinates used for distances

Map projections specify how geographic positions are represented on a plane (Snyder, 1987). Geographic location and distance are separate operations. Projected input uses its source distance units. For the WGS84 option, AquaProv converts degrees to a local metric approximation about the mean longitude λ₀ and latitude φ₀:

x = R(λ − λ₀)cos φ₀;   y = R(φ − φ₀)
R = 6,371,008.8 m; angles in radians
dᵢⱼ = √[(xᵢ − xⱼ)² + (yᵢ − yⱼ)²]

The local approximation is restricted to extents no greater than 3° in either direction and latitudes within ±85°. For larger or more precise studies, choose a suitable projected CRS and consistent distance units. Changing the basemap for display does not change the analysis distance coordinates. Duplicate locations and fewer than three non-collinear observations cannot define these interpolated surfaces without deliberate data preparation.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/maps/interpolation.py (planar_coordinates, validate_geometry).

Inverse-distance weighting (IDW)

At target s₀, IDW calculates a normalized weighted average of all retained sample values vᵢ. With distance dᵢ = ‖s₀ − sᵢ‖ and positive power p (Shepard, 1968):

wᵢ = dᵢ−p / ∑ⱼdⱼ−p
v̂(s₀) = ∑ᵢwᵢvᵢ

If the target exactly matches a sample, its observed value is returned. Internally distances are normalized by the nearest distance before exponentiation for numerical stability; normalized weights are unchanged. All weights are nonnegative and sum to 1, so predictions stay within the sample value range. Increasing p favours nearby samples more strongly. At distances 1 and 2 with p = 2, illustrative values 10 and 30 yield (10 + 30/4)/(1 + 1/4) = 14.

The grid is restricted to the convex hull, and to the study-boundary intersection when selected, unless boundary extrapolation is explicitly enabled. Grid resolution changes evaluation density, not the number of observations. IDW supplies no prediction variance or probability. Boundaries clip the display; they are not physical groundwater-flow barriers.

The current geographic gallery exports explicitly enable full-boundary coverage on a 120 × 120 grid. Predictions extend to the supplied Sukinda KML and are clipped at its exact outline; exported tables flag cells beyond the sample hull. The separate Liu seasonal study uses the convex hull of its 19 well locations as a labelled sampling envelope because no surveyed boundary was supplied for that study. Map labels use WGS84 longitude/latitude in decimal degrees, a true-north arrow and a geodesic kilometre scale; interpolation distances remain in local metres.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/maps/interpolation.py (idw, interpolate).

Ordinary kriging: model, weights and variance

Ordinary kriging assumes an unknown locally constant mean and an isotropic covariance depending only on distance. Let a be range, s the structured partial sill, and n₀ the nugget, all in consistent distance or squared measurement units. With r = d/a (Cressie, 1993):

Spherical C(d) = s(1 − 1.5r + 0.5r³) for r < 1; otherwise 0
Exponential C(d) = s exp(−3r)

For the exponential model, a is the practical range where structured covariance is s exp(−3), about 5% of s. Form Kᵢⱼ = C(dᵢⱼ) + n₀δᵢⱼ, cᵢ = C(‖sᵢ−s₀‖), and the ones vector 1. The unscaled equivalent of AquaProv's linear system is:

K w + 1μ = c;   1ᵀw = 1
v̂(s₀) = wᵀv
σ²pred(s₀) = s + n₀ − wᵀc − μ

μ is the Lagrange multiplier under this covariance sign convention. The implementation scales covariances by s+n₀ to improve numerical conditioning. The sum-to-one constraint preserves a constant mean. Weights may be negative, so predictions need not stay within observed values. The reported variance is for a new observation, including the nugget; with a nonzero nugget, a coincident target is not forced to reproduce an observed sample exactly.

The Maps module uses user-supplied range, partial sill and nugget and does not fit a variogram automatically. Fitted models are provided separately in Assessment. Neither module silently estimates anisotropy or removes a trend. Validate the model with held-out data before using its interpolated pattern scientifically.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/maps/interpolation.py (covariance, ordinary_kriging, interpolate).

Reading a prediction-variance map

Grid colour is σ²pred from the kriging system, in squared measurement units. Its square root is conditional prediction SD in measurement units. For fixed covariance parameters, the variance depends on locations and the covariance model, not directly on measured concentration magnitude. It can be high where spatial support is weak, but it does not measure pollution (Cressie, 1993).

This uncertainty is conditional on the selected variogram and stationarity assumptions; parameter uncertainty, missing processes and errors in coordinates are not included. The convex-hull mask limits the exported analysis domain. The standard Maps variance view does not independently establish a calibrated confidence interval or exceedance probability.

The supplied Sukinda study area#

The Sukinda worked data are attributed to their associated source publication (Sahoo et al., 2025). The Sukinda map examples use assets/data/Sukinda_CGWB_Data_Study_Area.kml, the study-area polygon supplied for this guide. It imports as WGS84 (EPSG:4326), named Study_Area. All 47 source sample coordinates fall inside the polygon.

Download study-area KML ↓
  1. Load the Sukinda workbook, select the longitude and latitude columns, and declare WGS84 as the source CRS.
  2. Under Study-area boundary, choose Import KML / KMZ / GeoJSON and select the supplied KML file. Enable Use study boundary.
  3. For the full-area IDW example, use WGS84 → local metres, power 2, resolution 120, Use only samples inside boundary, and Fill entire boundary (extrapolate).
  4. Keep Fill entire boundary enabled to reproduce the current gallery exports.

The KML is preserved unchanged. Its styling contains a legacy icon URL, but these maps read the polygon locally and make no network requests.

Sample locations#

  1. Import the coordinate table and select X = longitude/easting and Y = latitude/northing in Data.
  2. Choose the source coordinate system explicitly. Select a measurement for point colors and optionally a label column.
  3. Choose Plain coordinates, OpenStreetMap, Esri light gray, or Esri dark gray. Online backgrounds require internet.
  4. Generate the location view. Wait for the basemap status to finish; use Shift + scroll/drag to change the geographic extent.
  5. Export the current extent with the attribution and any incomplete-background warning intact.

Distance coordinates for interpolation#

Interpolation has a separate required Distance coordinates setting. Projected/local X/Y keeps the supplied distance units. WGS84 longitude/latitude is converted to local metres about the data centroid using an equirectangular approximation. The latter rejects extents wider than 3° in longitude or latitude and latitudes beyond ±85°. Use an appropriate projected CRS for a wider study.

Inputs must be finite and complete, with at least three distinct non-collinear sites and no duplicate locations. The interpolator accepts up to 1,000 samples and 20–160 grid cells per axis. Rows are not silently merged.

Ordinary kriging#

The Maps module uses isotropic ordinary kriging with an unknown constant mean and a sum-to-one weight constraint. You supply the covariance model, range, partial sill, and nugget. Default values are starting choices, not fitted estimates.

ParameterDefinition
Spherical modelCovariance vanishes at the specified support range.
Exponential modelC(h) = partial sill × exp(−3h/range); range is the practical range.
Partial sillPositive structured variance in squared measurement units.
NuggetNon-negative independent observation variance added to the covariance diagonal.
Prediction varianceConditional variance for a new observation, including the nugget; units are squared measurement units.

With zero nugget the prediction reproduces the sample values at their coordinates. Assessment offers a separate fitted-variogram and blocked-validation workflow. General reference: Cressie (1993).

IDW produces a deterministic weighted surface and does not supply a kriging variance or a statistical prediction interval. Reference: Shepard (1968).

Compare boundary coverage#

The current IDW example fills the entire supplied KML. The grid has 8,400 finite inside-boundary cells, including 1,547 beyond the sample hull. These cells are identified by the extrapolated flag in the numerical download. Cell counts depend on grid resolution and are not area percentages.

Study-area boundaries#

Use Study-area boundary to import KML, KMZ, or GeoJSON polygons, or draw/edit an area. Multiple polygons and holes are supported. Invalid geometry is rejected rather than silently repaired. Boundaries are embedded in saved projects and can be exported as GeoJSON.

Use study boundary enables clipping. Fill entire boundary (extrapolate) extends estimates beyond the sample convex hull; this control starts checked, so review it deliberately when applying a boundary. Extrapolated cells are flagged in results. Uncheck it to restrict coverage to the sampled area. Use only samples inside boundary controls whether outside samples can inform the interpolation.

A boundary is not a hydrogeological barrier

Polygon clipping changes display/coverage. It does not introduce flow barriers, directional covariance, or a transport model. Extrapolating to a study boundary can cover large unsampled areas.

Map presentation and backgrounds#

The contour controls can display the selected basemap below the surface, set surface opacity, show coordinate grids and contour labels, and position a vertical or horizontal color scale. True-north arrows require a declared source CRS; grid-north / +Y is a separate option. Labels may move or be omitted to avoid collisions; measured coordinates and grid values remain unchanged.

Online tile requests reveal your IP address and approximate viewed area to the selected provider. The application does not send the measurement table as a tile request. Plain coordinates makes no tile requests. Tile caches are bounded and offline background availability is not guaranteed.

Retain the provider attribution in every exported figure: OpenStreetMap contributors (n.d.) or Esri (n.d.). Tiles remain raster images inside PDF/SVG. Higher export DPI does not add map detail.

What to report#

  • Source coordinate system, distance conversion, selected campaign, excluded records, and any deliberate duplicate aggregation.
  • Interpolation method, model, range, partial sill, nugget, power, and grid resolution.
  • Boundary source, clipping, outside-sample policy, extrapolation status, and basemap provider.
  • Held-out errors and interval coverage if you claim predictive performance; see spatial validation.
