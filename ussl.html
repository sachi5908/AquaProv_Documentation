Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

From laboratory concentrations to chemical equivalentsSAR and conductivity-dependent USSL boundaries

From laboratory concentrations to chemical equivalents

A mass concentration gives the mass of an ion; an equivalent concentration also accounts for its electric charge. Let Cᵢ be concentration in mg/L, Mᵢ molar mass in g/mol (numerically also mg/mmol), and |zᵢ| the absolute ionic charge (Hem, 1985):

mᵢ = Cᵢ / Mᵢ (mmol/L)
eᵢ = |zᵢ| Cᵢ / Mᵢ (meq/L)

For an input already in meq/L, eᵢ is the supplied value. To recover mmol/L from meq/L, divide by |zᵢ|. These conversions are different for divalent ions such as Ca²⁺ and monovalent ions such as Na⁺.

Ion Mᵢ (g/mol) Absolute charge
Ca 40.0780 2
Mg 24.3050 2
Na 22.9898 1
K 39.0983 1
HCO₃ 61.0171 1
CO₃ 60.0092 2
Cl 35.4527 1
SO₄ 96.0636 2

Illustrative check: 40.078 mg/L Ca is 1 mmol/L and 2 meq/L; 22.9898 mg/L Na is 1 mmol/L and 1 meq/L. Equal molar concentrations do not mean equal charge contributions. Concentrations must be finite and nonnegative. A missing measurement does not become a measured zero; check the selected missing-data policy and any explicitly supplied values.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/ions.py (ions_WEIGHT, ions_CHARGE), aquaprovpy/_chemistry.py (major_ion_meq).

SAR and conductivity-dependent USSL boundaries

Using Na, Ca and Mg in meq/L, AquaProv calculates sodium adsorption ratio (SAR) (U.S. Salinity Laboratory Staff, 1954):

SAR = eNa / √[(eCa + eMg)/2]

Ca+Mg must be positive. The horizontal axis is measured EC referenced to 25 °C, expressed in µS/cm; 1 mS/cm = 1 dS/m = 1,000 µS/cm. No TDS-to-EC estimate or temperature correction is performed. The salinity class changes at EC = 250, 750 and 2,250 µS/cm. Sodium-hazard boundaries are curves, with E denoting the numerical EC value in µS/cm:

S1/S2: SAR = 18.87 − 4.44 log₁₀E
S2/S3: SAR = 31.31 − 6.66 log₁₀E
S3/S4: SAR = 43.75 − 8.87 log₁₀E

Each crossed boundary increases the sodium class; exact ties use the higher class. The combined reference domain is 100 ≤ EC ≤ 5,000 and 0 ≤ SAR ≤ 32. Values outside it are reported explicitly. Review flags mark points within 0.5 SAR of a sodium curve or 25 µS/cm of a salinity cut; these are review tolerances, not uncertainty intervals.

Illustrative check: Ca = 2, Mg = 2 and Na = 2 meq/L give SAR = 1.414. At EC = 1,000, the S1/S2 boundary is 5.55 and the point is C3–S1. Field suitability also depends on soil, crop, drainage and irrigation management.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/irrigation.py (calculate, ussl_boundaries, classify_ussl).

How it works#

The horizontal axis is measured EC in µS/cm, referenced to 25 °C. The vertical axis is SAR calculated from Na, Ca, and Mg in meq/L. Salinity classes C1–C4 and sodium-hazard classes S1–S4 use historical reference boundaries.

SAR = Na / √[(Ca + Mg)/2]

Reference: U.S. Salinity Laboratory Staff (1954).

Read the result#

Interpretation limits#

Keep the measurement context

EC is not estimated from TDS or corrected for temperature. Historical graphical classes do not establish suitability for a particular soil, crop, irrigation practice, or drainage condition.

Boundary provenance#

USSL C-class cutoffs are 250, 750, and 2,250 µS/cm. The plotted reference domain is EC 100–5,000 µS/cm and SAR 0–32. Outside-domain samples remain visible and receive an explicit status. Conductivity-only C class remains tabulated.

S1/S2 = 18.87 − 4.44 log10(EC)
S2/S3 = 31.31 − 6.66 log10(EC)
S3/S4 = 43.75 − 8.87 log10(EC)EC is in µS/cm. Equations from Handbook 60, p. 79; diagram p. 80.

Exact boundary equality is assigned to the higher-hazard/right-hand region. Boundary_review checks proximity using 25 µS/cm, 0.5 SAR, or one Na percentage point, as applicable. These are review heuristics, not confidence intervals.
