Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

Thermodynamic definition of mineral saturation index

For the mineral dissolution reaction written in the selected PHREEQC database, species activities determine the ion activity product (IAP). Let νⱼ be reaction stoichiometric exponents and K(T) its equilibrium constant at the solution temperature (Parkhurst & Appelo, 2013):

IAP = ∏ⱼaⱼνⱼ
SI = log₁₀[IAP/K(T)] = ∑ⱼνⱼlog₁₀aⱼ − log₁₀K(T)
aⱼ = γⱼmⱼ (on the database's activity standard-state basis)

Here mⱼ is species molality and γⱼ the activity coefficient; water activity enters where required by the database reaction. For calcite dissolution CaCO₃(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻, IAP = aCa²⁺aCO₃²⁻. Total calcium and total inorganic carbon are not automatically the free-species activities. For dolomite, the analogous product is aCa²⁺aMg²⁺aCO₃²⁻². PHREEQC uses each phase's actual database reaction; AquaProv does not hard-code an approximate concentration product.

Illustrative check: IAP/K = 0.1 gives SI = −1; a ratio of 1 gives SI = 0; a ratio of 10 gives SI = +1. One SI unit is a tenfold change in the saturation ratio. Negative SI permits dissolution if the mineral is present and accessible; positive SI indicates thermodynamic precipitation potential, which may be inhibited by kinetics or nucleation. SI is not mineral abundance or a reaction rate.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/backend.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/engine.py.

Background calculation: aqueous speciation and activities

In calculation mode AquaProv writes the mapped elemental totals, measured pH and solution conditions to IPhreeqc. PHREEQC solves aqueous mass-action and component-balance equations under those inputs; the selected database controls complexes, equilibrium constants and the activity model. Ionic strength, I = ½∑ⱼmⱼzⱼ², links the full dissolved composition to activity coefficients. Thus omitting major ions can affect the SI of a mineral even when its own components are measured (Charlton & Parkhurst, 2011; Parkhurst & Appelo, 2013).

pH constrains hydrogen activity by pH = −log₁₀aH⁺. Alkalinity or DIC constrains carbonate chemistry; bicarbonate concentration is not silently treated as a titrated alkalinity. Density relates volume-based inputs to solvent-mass concentrations. Temperature changes equilibrium constants and species distributions. For an explicitly supplied Eh versus the standard hydrogen electrode, pe = F·Eh/(ln(10)RT), with Eh in volts and T in kelvin. Missing redox information cannot be inferred merely from a total-iron concentration.

The workflow speciates initial solutions at 1 atm. It neither equilibrates the water with selected minerals nor adjusts an ion to force charge balance. The worked dataset uses phreeqc.dat, 25 °C, density 1 kg/L and TA as CaCO₃; these are stated assumptions, not all measured properties. Review Mineral SI inputs and reporting bases before changing databases or using saline/redox-sensitive water.

In import mode, the supplied SI values are preserved without repeating this calculation. A database/version, source rounding and activity model cannot be recovered from the SI column alone. Undefined PHREEQC sentinels −999.999 and −999 become unassessed, not extreme undersaturation.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/backend.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/automatic.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/data.py (import_si).

Exact classes and mineral-specific sample counts

A finite SI < 0 is Undersaturated; SI = 0 is At equilibrium; SI > 0 is Supersaturated. The classifier uses full numerical values with no hidden tolerance or rounding. A label rounded to 0.00 may therefore still be negative or positive. A zero in imported data may also reflect rounding by its source.

Per-mineral summaries use finite SI values to calculate mean, median, sample SD (denominator n−1), quartiles and extremes. Class percentage = 100 × class count / that mineral's assessed count. Missing SI is excluded from this denominator and reported separately. For 8 assessed values with 6 supersaturated and 2 undersaturated, supersaturation is 75%, even if two other source records were unassessed. Comparing minerals requires checking their potentially different assessed counts.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/data.py (classify, summarize).

Coordinates used for distances

Map projections specify how geographic positions are represented on a plane (Snyder, 1987). Geographic location and distance are separate operations. Projected input uses its source distance units. For WGS84 longitude / latitude → local metres, AquaProv converts degrees to a local metric approximation about the mean longitude λ₀ and latitude φ₀:

x = R(λ − λ₀)cos φ₀;   y = R(φ − φ₀)
R = 6,371,008.8 m; angles in radians
dᵢⱼ = √[(xᵢ − xⱼ)² + (yᵢ − yⱼ)²]

The local approximation is restricted to extents no greater than 3° in either direction and latitudes within ±85°. For larger or more precise studies, choose a suitable projected CRS and consistent distance units. Changing the basemap for display does not change the analysis distance coordinates. Duplicate locations and fewer than three non-collinear observations cannot define these interpolated surfaces without deliberate data preparation.

The current Distance coordinates control also offers automatic UTM/UPS and a custom projected CRS in metres. These transform WGS84 positions through the selected projection before calculating distances; they do not use the local equation above. Enter Analysis CRS for the custom mode, and retain the selected CRS in the calculation record. For existing projected X/Y, Source CRS override declares the original projected system.

Choose Grid definition → Cells per axis for 20–1,000 cells per axis, or Cell size in analysis units for explicit spacing in the analysis coordinate units. The cell-size option has a 1,000,000-point limit. Resolution changes where predictions are evaluated; it does not increase sample support. Colour classification and Surface drawing change the presentation. Retain exported class boundaries when comparing Equal intervals, Quantiles, Natural Breaks (Jenks) or custom classes; large-grid Natural Breaks uses a bounded histogram approximation.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/maps/interpolation.py (planar_coordinates, validate_geometry).

Inverse-distance weighting (IDW)

At target s₀, IDW calculates a normalized weighted average of the selected neighbouring sample values vᵢ. Nearest neighbours → All samples includes all eligible observations; a positive count selects the nearest available observations and includes ties at the cutoff distance. With distance dᵢ = ‖s₀ − sᵢ‖ and positive power p, the sums run over that selected set (Shepard, 1968):

wᵢ = dᵢ−p / ∑ⱼdⱼ−p
v̂(s₀) = ∑ᵢwᵢvᵢ

If the target exactly matches a sample, its observed value is returned. Internally distances are normalized by the nearest distance before exponentiation for numerical stability; normalized weights are unchanged. All weights are nonnegative and sum to 1, so predictions stay within the sample value range. Increasing p favours nearby samples more strongly. At distances 1 and 2 with p = 2, illustrative values 10 and 30 yield (10 + 30/4)/(1 + 1/4) = 14.

The grid is restricted to the convex hull, and to the study-boundary intersection when selected, unless boundary extrapolation is explicitly enabled. Grid resolution changes evaluation density, not the number of observations. IDW supplies no prediction variance or probability. Boundaries clip the display; they are not physical groundwater-flow barriers.

The current geographic gallery exports explicitly enable full-boundary coverage on a 120 × 120 grid. Predictions extend to the supplied Sukinda KML and are clipped at its exact outline; exported tables flag cells beyond the sample hull. The separate Liu seasonal study uses the convex hull of its 19 well locations as a labelled sampling envelope because no surveyed boundary was supplied for that study. Map labels use WGS84 longitude/latitude in decimal degrees, a true-north arrow and a geodesic kilometre scale; interpolation distances remain in local metres.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/maps/interpolation.py (idw, interpolate).

Ordinary kriging: model, weights and variance

Ordinary kriging assumes an unknown locally constant mean and an isotropic covariance depending only on distance. Let a be range, s the structured partial sill, and n₀ the nugget, all in consistent distance or squared measurement units. With r = d/a (Cressie, 1993):

Spherical C(d) = s(1 − 1.5r + 0.5r³) for r < 1; otherwise 0
Exponential C(d) = s exp(−3r)

For the exponential model, a is the practical range where structured covariance is s exp(−3), about 5% of s. Form Kᵢⱼ = C(dᵢⱼ) + n₀δᵢⱼ, cᵢ = C(‖sᵢ−s₀‖), and the ones vector 1. The unscaled equivalent of AquaProv's linear system is:

K w + 1μ = c;   1ᵀw = 1
v̂(s₀) = wᵀv
σ²pred(s₀) = s + n₀ − wᵀc − μ

μ is the Lagrange multiplier under this covariance sign convention. The implementation scales covariances by s+n₀ to improve numerical conditioning. The sum-to-one constraint preserves a constant mean. Weights may be negative, so predictions need not stay within observed values. The reported variance is for a new observation, including the nugget; with a nonzero nugget, a coincident target is not forced to reproduce an observed sample exactly.

The Maps module uses user-supplied range, partial sill and nugget and does not fit a variogram automatically. Fitted models are provided separately in Assessment. Neither module silently estimates anisotropy or removes a trend. Validate the model with held-out data before using its interpolated pattern scientifically.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/maps/interpolation.py (covariance, ordinary_kriging, interpolate).

Interpolate a logarithmic saturation index

The selected mineral's finite sample SI values are passed to IDW or ordinary kriging after coordinate preparation. The worked figure uses Calcite, IDW power 2 and a 120 × 120 grid covering the full supplied Sukinda KML, with extrapolated cells flagged in the export. Geographic labels, true north and a geodesic scale accompany the map. Interpolation acts directly on SI, not on free-ion activities, concentration inputs or mineral abundance.

For IDW weights wᵢ, this has a useful logarithmic interpretation:

SÎ = ∑ᵢwᵢSIᵢ
10SÎ = ∏ᵢ(10SIᵢ)wᵢ

Thus IDW averages log saturation ratios, equivalent to a weighted geometric mean of the sample ratios. It does not speciate a physically mixed water, and it is not generally equal to calculating SI after interpolating chemistry. Grid classification uses the sign of predicted SI; the SI = 0 contour marks an interpolated threshold, not a demonstrated reaction front. All interpretation remains conditional on data coverage, the interpolation method and original SI assumptions.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/plots.py (_spatial, view='interpolation'), aquaprovpy/modules/maps/interpolation.py.

Source data & selection#

All 47 source samples retained in the audit. PHREEQC calculation with phreeqc.dat: Calcite, Dolomite, Gypsum, Anhydrite, Halite and Fluorite. Incomplete chemistry at samples 30 and 31 is excluded from speciation; zero fluoride activity can leave additional mineral results undefined (Sahoo et al., 2025).

Automatic chemistry mapping; temperature 25 °C, density 1 kg/L, TA as CaCO3. No measured redox constraint. These are documented demonstration assumptions, not independently reviewed laboratory conditions.

Reproduce it in AquaProv

  1. Open Mineral SI and select SI interpolated map in the left Plot library. Use its search box to find the plot.
  2. Use Load data in the top toolbar, Ctrl + I, or Data → Dataset → Import CSV or Excel. Import the exact input linked on this page. Select the intended worksheet for a multi-sheet workbook and check the row count and headers in the Data inspector.
  3. Turn Auto update off while entering the worked choices. In the right inspector, open the named tab and click a section heading to expand it; scroll down for the lower controls.
  4. Open Data → Mineral saturation data → Source. Keep Automatic: detect SI or water chemistry for the Sukinda chemistry example; it uses recognized SI columns when present and otherwise calculates with PHREEQC. Choose the sample/event identifier.
  5. Open Data → PHREEQC solution settings. The worked calculation uses phreeqc.dat, the measured pH column, constant temperature 25 °C and density 1 kg/L. Use Map dissolved chemistry and units… to review ion mappings and TA as CaCO3. Leave unmeasured redox unconstrained and review the incomplete-chemistry exclusion policy.
  6. For a manual calculation, choose Source → Calculate with PHREEQC and confirm I reviewed units, chemical basis and solution conditions. For imported SI, choose Import PHREEQC saturation indices and review mineral/column mappings and database provenance.
  7. Open Data → Minerals, samples and groups to select Calcite, Dolomite, Gypsum, Anhydrite, Halite and Fluorite. Use Compare samples… for sample IDs 1–12 in the bar example; retain all selected samples in the other examples. Review the Group / season column and Show group / season filter.
  8. Open Settings → SI figure settings for the mineral(s) and view-specific display. The mineral scatter uses Dolomite on X and Calcite on Y; maps show Calcite. A fixed symmetric SI colour scale helps compare figures; missing SI stays unassessed.
  9. Open Data → Sample locations. Select X = longitude / easting, Y = latitude / northing and the declared Source coordinate system. For the prepared geographic examples, choose Longitude, Latitude and WGS84 longitude / latitude (EPSG:4326). Choose Plain coordinates (no background) for an offline background.
  10. When the worked example uses a boundary, open Data → Study-area boundary → Import KML / KMZ / GeoJSON…, select the linked file and check Use study boundary. Otherwise leave it off. For interpolation, match Use only samples inside boundary and Fill entire boundary (extrapolate) to the worked settings; review the support flags in the exported grid.
  11. Open Settings → SI spatial interpolation and choose Distance coordinates. The worked local-metre examples use WGS84 longitude / latitude → local metres. The current application also offers WGS84 → automatic UTM / UPS (metres) and WGS84 → custom projected CRS (metres); the latter reveals Analysis CRS. Use a declared projected system for larger areas.
  12. Choose Method under Settings → SI spatial interpolation. In Settings → SI spatial interpolation, IDW uses IDW power and Nearest neighbours (All samples uses every eligible observation). Ordinary kriging reveals Variogram, range, partial sill and nugget fields, with their units. Enter the worked values; starting defaults are not fitted estimates.
  13. In Settings → SI spatial interpolation, choose Grid definition → Cells per axis and set Grid cells per axis, or select Cell size in analysis units and enter Cell size (distance units). Use the grid stated for this example.
  14. In Settings → SI spatial interpolation, use Colour classification for Automatic contour intervals, Equal intervals, Quantiles, Natural Breaks (Jenks) or Custom class boundaries. The custom option reveals Class boundaries. Surface drawing selects filled contours or classified raster cells. Display classes do not change scientific index thresholds.
  15. Settings → SI spatial interpolation → Apply WGS84 local IDW preset is an optional starting configuration: automatic UTM/UPS, 20-metre cells, 12 nearest observations, power 2, five Natural Breaks classes and raster drawing. It changes several settings together; inspect them before Generate. The older worked figures use their stated grids and distance conversion.
  16. Open Settings → Map layout for the colour scale controls available for this view. Open Settings → Coordinates and grid for frame coordinates, labelled borders, geographic label format, spacing and line design. Under Settings → North arrow, enable Show north arrow and choose Arrow reference → True north (requires source CRS) for geographic north, or Grid north / +Y for the grid direction.
  17. Open Settings → Scale bar, enable Show scale bar, and choose units, total length, divisions, design and position. When the CRS is unknown, declare the distance unit for local X/Y. Generate and check coverage, labels, north and distance scale together.
  18. Click Generate figure or Generate map, or press F5. Open Export → Results & methods to review exclusions, diagnostics, numerical tables and methods before interpreting or exporting the figure.

Read the figure#

Read SI relative to zero: negative is undersaturated, positive is supersaturated, and an exact zero is equilibrium. Saturation does not establish mineral abundance or precipitation rate. Unassessed cells are not zero. All figures use the selected database and stated solution conditions.

Refine the figure style

  1. In the right inspector, open Settings → Plot settings. Use Show plot title and Manual plot title (optional); leave the title blank for the automatic title. Set title size, weight, style and position here.
  2. Open Style → Publication figure → Typography. Choose the font family and annotation, axis-label, tick and legend sizes. The top toolbar also provides quick typography controls.
  3. In Style → Publication figure, open Labels & ticks to edit axis labels, visibility, limits, ticks and tick formats. Enter the colourbar title in Color scale label (blank = original). Keep quantity and units in labels; blank labels and limits retain the original labels and automatic bounds.
  4. In the same Publication figure panel, open Canvas & grid to set backgrounds, axis borders, grid visibility and layout padding. For geographic frames, use the map-specific Settings → Coordinates and grid controls when available.
  5. Open Style → Publication figure → Legend & color scale. Set Legend position (also draggable), columns, frame and individual Legend items for this plot. Choose Colour scale, Show colourbar and colourbar text sizes for numeric colours. Sample plots need a numeric colour column for a continuous scale; categorical groups use their group colours. Normalize colour scale to 0–1 changes display labels while retaining measurements and scientific classifications.
  6. Open Style → Publication figure → Dimensions & export. Choose Figure sizing → Custom, then set Width (inches) and Height (inches). Review Transparent background and Crop to content; cropping can change exported dimensions.
  7. Click Apply style and inspect the figure at its intended final size. Save preset and Load preset reuse presentation settings. Method, variable, grouping, sample-symbol and interpolation changes require Generate when Auto update is off.

Export and preserve

  1. Finish the calculation with Generate figure or Generate map (or F5). Read the warnings, then apply the final style.
  2. Open Export → Figure export. Enter File name, use Save to → Browse to choose your output folder, then select File format and Image resolution (DPI). Use distinct names for alternative results.
  3. Click the format-specific button, such as Export PNG, Export SVG or Export PDF. Open the saved file and check labels, legends, dimensions and the current view. Raster pixel dimensions depend on figure size and DPI.
  4. Open Export → Results & methods. Select a result table to inspect it. Choose Report format: CSV + JSON, Excel (.xlsx), Word (.docx) or PDF (.pdf), then click its export button. Available tables depend on the plot; some geochemical diagrams do not expose a table for every graphical element.
  5. Press Ctrl + S to save the .aquaprov project, including its data and per-plot settings. Keep the original measurements, exported results and final figure together.
