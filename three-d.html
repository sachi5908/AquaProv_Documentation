Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

XYZ scatter in source-variable coordinates

A three-dimensional scatterplot represents an observation by its position on three numerical axes (Cleveland, 1993). Each point is exactly (Xᵢ, Yᵢ, Zᵢ) from three selected numeric columns. Colour follows Z over the measurement range. AquaProv applies no CRS conversion, standardization, regression or PCA in this mode. All coordinate/measurement values must be finite; the scientific meaning of distance depends on the chosen variables and their units. Reference lines to the coordinate planes and camera changes are graphical aids, not additional measurements.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/three_d/plots.py (plot_xyz).

Piecewise-linear XYZ interpolation

The surface triangulates the observed X–Y coordinates with Delaunay triangles. Within one triangle, a target is a barycentric combination of its three vertices (Amidror, 2002):

(x,y) = α₁(x₁,y₁) + α₂(x₂,y₂) + α₃(x₃,y₃)
α₁ + α₂ + α₃ = 1;   αᵢ ≥ 0
ẑ(x,y) = α₁z₁ + α₂z₂ + α₃z₃

Thus each triangle is a plane passing through the three observations. For equal barycentric weights and vertex Z values 10, 20, 30, the target value is 20. Refinement produces smaller render triangles on the same piecewise-linear surface; it does not add observations or fit a smoother model. Outside the sample convex hull values remain undefined. Duplicate or collinear X–Y locations must be resolved. X and Y remain in source units; no geographic distance correction is inferred. The surface provides no prediction variance.

For the updated Sukinda documentation figure, the exporter evaluates this linear model within the sample hull and explicitly extends the outer band to the KML using the nearest observation in local metric coordinates. Its render mesh is clipped to the exact polygon. The exported table identifies extrapolated cells. Longitude/latitude axes and a plan-view inset provide geographic labels, true north and a geodesic scale. This documented extension is implemented in docs/scripts/refresh_map_figures.py; it adds no observations and does not change the generic 3D module's default model.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/modules/three_d/plots.py (plot_xyz, SciPy LinearNDInterpolator and Delaunay triangulation).

The statistical analysis matrix

Let X contain n retained samples (rows) and p explicitly selected measurements (columns). IDs and coordinate columns should only enter if they are the scientific variables being studied. The default rejects missing measurements and constant columns; explicit drop policies use complete rows across the selected variables and record exclusions. There is no automatic imputation, logarithm, outlier removal or conversion from the ion-unit selector.

For standardized analyses, AquaProv subtracts each variable's mean and divides by its sample standard deviation (Jolliffe & Cadima, 2016):

x̄ⱼ = (1/n)∑ᵢ xᵢⱼ
sⱼ = √[∑ᵢ(xᵢⱼ − x̄ⱼ)²/(n − 1)]
zᵢⱼ = (xᵢⱼ − x̄ⱼ)/sⱼ

Center-only PCA/HCA uses xᵢⱼ − x̄ⱼ without division. The standardized sample heatmap and standardized boxplots always use sample z-scores; the scatter matrix displays original units. For the illustrative values 1, 2, 3, the mean is 2, sample SD is 1 and z-scores are −1, 0, 1. Changing the retained rows changes these preprocessing quantities.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/_publication.py (numeric_frame, standardize), aquaprovpy/multivariate.py.

PCA by singular value decomposition

For the centered (optionally standardized) n × p matrix Z, singular value decomposition gives Z = U D Vᵀ. Let dₖ be singular value k and vₖ its variable direction (Jolliffe & Cadima, 2016):

λₖ = dₖ²/(n − 1)
Scores T = Z Vretained
Loadings L = Vretained diag(√λₖ)
Explained variance (%) = 100λₖ / ∑ⱼλⱼ

The retained component count cannot exceed the matrix rank. AquaProv fixes otherwise arbitrary signs by making the largest absolute eigenvector entry positive. Scores locate samples; loadings describe variable relationships with components. Loadings are not the eigenvectors alone. With standardized data, they are correlations between each variable and unrotated PC scores; covariance PCA loadings retain variable-scale effects.

For optional orthogonal Varimax, each loading row is divided by its retained norm (Kaiser normalization), an orthogonal matrix R maximizes the normalized loading simplicity criterion, then row scales are restored (Kaiser, 1958):

Lrot = L R
Trot = T diag(1/√λₖ) R
Rotated variance (%) for k = 100∑ⱼLrot,jk² / ∑ⱼλⱼ

The rotated score coordinates have unit sample variance; their axis percentages come from rotated loadings. Rotation preserves retained communalities and total retained variance, but its axes are not the original ordered PCs. PCA finds variance directions, not causes or proven source contributions. Review preprocessing, retained variance and sample adequacy with the score plot.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/multivariate.py (pca, varimax, plot).

Three-dimensional sample scores

Each point has coordinates (Tᵢ₁, Tᵢ₂, Tᵢ₃), or the first three unit-variance rotated scores. At least three retained components are required. AquaProv uses equal coordinate units across the three axes and reports the sum of their variance percentages. Camera rotation and perspective change the projection on screen, not the calculated coordinates. Apparent proximity from one camera angle can hide separation along the third axis or omitted components.

Three-dimensional component loadings

Each variable ends at (Lⱼ₁, Lⱼ₂, Lⱼ₃), or its first three rotated loadings. Optional lines connect these endpoints to the origin. For standardized PCA the squared vector length is the fraction of the variable's variance represented by these three components. For covariance PCA the scale is variable-dependent. Vector direction shows association with the displayed components; it does not specify reaction direction or a physical transport vector. Camera controls only change the view.

XYZ surfaces#

The surface uses Delaunay triangles and piecewise-linear interpolation. Measured peaks and the sample-hull boundary are retained. Mesh resolution controls refinement and the exported rectangular grid, not smoothing. At least three distinct non-collinear locations are required. The default surface opacity is 1.

Interpret the axes literally

XYZ plots do not infer a CRS, depth sign, vertical exaggeration, or a volumetric subsurface model. A surface of concentration above a coordinate plane is a visualization of concentration.

PCA in three dimensions#

PCA axes use equal coordinate scales. Three displayed axes may represent less variance than all retained components. Loading endpoints have numbered labels and a variable key; label relocation does not jitter their coordinates. See the PCA guide for definitions and Varimax score conventions.

Camera and coordinate guides#

  • Isometric and XY/XZ/YZ presets use orthographic projection. The collapsed axis is hidden in a plane view.
  • Auto rotate changes only the camera. Its View menu controls speed, direction, exact angles, and projection.
  • Dragging, changing plots, resetting, generating, exporting, or leaving the module stops automatic rotation. Image export records a stationary view.
  • XYZ coordinate guides connect samples to the coordinate planes at the first axis limits; they do not force coordinates to zero. PCA guides refer to the origin.

Prepare a readable 3D figure#

Save complementary plane views when overlap hides relationships. Report selected variables, units, projection, and the displayed component variance where applicable. Keep measured sample markers visible on an interpolated surface when they help readers distinguish measurements from the mesh.
