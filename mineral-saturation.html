Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

Thermodynamic definition of mineral saturation index

For the mineral dissolution reaction written in the selected PHREEQC database, species activities determine the ion activity product (IAP). Let νⱼ be reaction stoichiometric exponents and K(T) its equilibrium constant at the solution temperature (Parkhurst & Appelo, 2013):

IAP = ∏ⱼaⱼνⱼ
SI = log₁₀[IAP/K(T)] = ∑ⱼνⱼlog₁₀aⱼ − log₁₀K(T)
aⱼ = γⱼmⱼ (on the database's activity standard-state basis)

Here mⱼ is species molality and γⱼ the activity coefficient; water activity enters where required by the database reaction. For calcite dissolution CaCO₃(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻, IAP = aCa²⁺aCO₃²⁻. Total calcium and total inorganic carbon are not automatically the free-species activities. For dolomite, the analogous product is aCa²⁺aMg²⁺aCO₃²⁻². PHREEQC uses each phase's actual database reaction; AquaProv does not hard-code an approximate concentration product.

Illustrative check: IAP/K = 0.1 gives SI = −1; a ratio of 1 gives SI = 0; a ratio of 10 gives SI = +1. One SI unit is a tenfold change in the saturation ratio. Negative SI permits dissolution if the mineral is present and accessible; positive SI indicates thermodynamic precipitation potential, which may be inhibited by kinetics or nucleation. SI is not mineral abundance or a reaction rate.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/backend.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/engine.py.

Background calculation: aqueous speciation and activities

In calculation mode AquaProv writes the mapped elemental totals, measured pH and solution conditions to IPhreeqc. PHREEQC solves aqueous mass-action and component-balance equations under those inputs; the selected database controls complexes, equilibrium constants and the activity model. Ionic strength, I = ½∑ⱼmⱼzⱼ², links the full dissolved composition to activity coefficients. Thus omitting major ions can affect the SI of a mineral even when its own components are measured (Charlton & Parkhurst, 2011; Parkhurst & Appelo, 2013).

pH constrains hydrogen activity by pH = −log₁₀aH⁺. Alkalinity or DIC constrains carbonate chemistry; bicarbonate concentration is not silently treated as a titrated alkalinity. Density relates volume-based inputs to solvent-mass concentrations. Temperature changes equilibrium constants and species distributions. For an explicitly supplied Eh versus the standard hydrogen electrode, pe = F·Eh/(ln(10)RT), with Eh in volts and T in kelvin. Missing redox information cannot be inferred merely from a total-iron concentration.

The workflow speciates initial solutions at 1 atm. It neither equilibrates the water with selected minerals nor adjusts an ion to force charge balance. The worked dataset uses phreeqc.dat, 25 °C, density 1 kg/L and TA as CaCO₃; these are stated assumptions, not all measured properties. Review Mineral SI inputs and reporting bases before changing databases or using saline/redox-sensitive water.

In import mode, the supplied SI values are preserved without repeating this calculation. A database/version, source rounding and activity model cannot be recovered from the SI column alone. Undefined PHREEQC sentinels −999.999 and −999 become unassessed, not extreme undersaturation.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/backend.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/automatic.py, aquaprovpy/saturation/data.py (import_si).

Exact classes and mineral-specific sample counts

A finite SI < 0 is Undersaturated; SI = 0 is At equilibrium; SI > 0 is Supersaturated. The classifier uses full numerical values with no hidden tolerance or rounding. A label rounded to 0.00 may therefore still be negative or positive. A zero in imported data may also reflect rounding by its source.

Per-mineral summaries use finite SI values to calculate mean, median, sample SD (denominator n−1), quartiles and extremes. Class percentage = 100 × class count / that mineral's assessed count. Missing SI is excluded from this denominator and reported separately. For 8 assessed values with 6 supersaturated and 2 undersaturated, supersaturation is 75%, even if two other source records were unassessed. Comparing minerals requires checking their potentially different assessed counts.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/data.py (classify, summarize).

Interpolate a logarithmic saturation index

The selected mineral's finite sample SI values are passed to IDW or ordinary kriging after coordinate preparation. The worked figure uses Calcite, IDW power 2 and a 120 × 120 grid covering the full supplied Sukinda KML, with extrapolated cells flagged in the export. Geographic labels, true north and a geodesic scale accompany the map. Interpolation acts directly on SI, not on free-ion activities, concentration inputs or mineral abundance.

For IDW weights wᵢ, this has a useful logarithmic interpretation:

SÎ = ∑ᵢwᵢSIᵢ
10SÎ = ∏ᵢ(10SIᵢ)wᵢ

Thus IDW averages log saturation ratios, equivalent to a weighted geometric mean of the sample ratios. It does not speciate a physically mixed water, and it is not generally equal to calculating SI after interpolating chemistry. Grid classification uses the sign of predicted SI; the SI = 0 contour marks an interpolated threshold, not a demonstrated reaction front. All interpretation remains conditional on data coverage, the interpolation method and original SI assumptions.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/saturation/plots.py (_spatial, view='interpolation'), aquaprovpy/modules/maps/interpolation.py.

Automatic calculation after import#

Load an Excel/CSV file in AquaProv and open Mineral SI. The default Automatic: detect SI or water chemistry source uses existing SI columns when present, including recognized long-format Mineral/SI tables. Otherwise it maps recognized chemistry and runs the bundled PHREEQC engine. Auto update starts the calculation when the module opens or its data changes. If Auto update is off, click Generate figure. You do not need to calculate SI in another program or tick the manual-review checkbox in Automatic mode.

Automatic setup selects the common minerals supported by the mapped components, excludes incomplete mapped samples with an audit record, and recognizes labelled mass/molar/equivalent units. For aqueous tables, ppm/ppb are interpreted as mg/L/µg/L; unlabelled concentrations assume mg/L. Measured temperature/density columns are used when recognized; otherwise the displayed defaults are 25 °C and 1 kg/L. TA/alkalinity assumes a CaCO3 reporting basis unless another supported basis is labelled. These assumptions are visible in Data and exported in Results & methods; automatic setup is not recorded as user-reviewed chemistry. Edit mappings, units, conditions or the missing-data policy as needed.

Automatic setup does not invent missing concentrations, silently substitute censored values, turn HCO3 into a titrated alkalinity measurement, or invent a redox constraint. Silica and iron-oxide phases remain unselected when their required inputs are unavailable. Recognized longitude/latitude columns select WGS84 distances for SI maps. Existing SI values are never recalculated automatically. Explicit Import/Calculate choices and settings persist in saved projects; mappings from a different dataset are discarded.

Projects saved before Automatic mode existed have their old unconditional Import default upgraded to Automatic. Their SI mappings remain available, and loading a later chemistry file can switch to calculation. Reviewed calculation settings remain unchanged.

For Sukinda_CGWB_Data_2024.xlsx, Automatic mode selects Calcite, Dolomite, Gypsum, Anhydrite, Halite and Fluorite. Samples 30 and 31 are excluded because NO3 and F are missing, respectively. Zero activity can make individual mineral SI undefined even in a complete sample. Review the displayed temperature, density and alkalinity assumptions before scientific interpretation.

Import existing results#

Use Import data for CSV or Excel. CSV files may use commas, semicolons or tabs, including PHREEQC selected output saved with a .csv extension. Choose the worksheet in an Excel workbook.

  • Wide format: one row per sample/event, with columns such as Sample, si_Calcite, si_Dolomite and si_Gypsum. Calcite, SI(Calcite) and Calcite SI are also recognized. Review imported mineral → SI columns maps other headings explicitly.
  • Long format: choose the sample/event identifier, mineral-name column and SI column. Repeated sample/mineral pairs must have distinct event identifiers or be separated by the group/season filter before sample comparisons.
  • Enter the original database/version in Imported database provenance when known. Imported values are not recalculated or attributed to AquaProv's selected calculation database. PHREEQC's undefined SI sentinel -999.999 (and the common exported -999) becomes unassessed; blank cells remain missing.

The source file hash, worksheet and source-row positions accompany results. A zero in an imported table may reflect upstream rounding; original activities cannot be recovered from SI alone.

Calculate from groundwater chemistry#

Automatic mode normally selects this path for you. Choose Calculate with PHREEQC explicitly for the manual review workflow below. The Windows x64 distribution includes the actual USGS IPhreeqc 3.7.3 engine and the matching phreeqc.dat and wateq4f.dat databases. Choose another thermodynamic database accepts a compatible .dat file, including a suitable pitzer.dat, sit.dat or other reviewed database. Custom database text and its SHA-256 digest are embedded in the project. Names and constants are used exactly as supplied; missing phases are reported rather than substituted with a different polymorph.

  1. Map measured pH, on the hydrogen-ion activity scale. Choose a temperature column in °C or review the explicit constant (initially 25 °C).
  2. Review solution density in kg/L; the initial constant of 1 kg/L is a freshwater approximation. Pressure is 1 atm. This workflow is for initial aqueous speciation, not elevated-pressure or reaction-path simulations.
  3. Open Map dissolved chemistry and units. Include the measured major ions that determine ionic strength, plus the dissolved components needed for the minerals of interest. Suggestions require review. Mass units are mg/L or µg/L; molar units are mmol/L or mol/L. meq/L is supported for fixed-charge ions and alkalinity, not total Fe, DIC or silica.
  4. Select the reporting basis: sulfate as SO4 or S, nitrate as NO3 or N, silica as SiO2 or Si, and so on. PHREEQC uses the formula weights in the selected database. For alkalinity as CaCO3, AquaProv passes the explicit equivalent formula Ca0.5(CO3)0.5.
  5. Use measured alkalinity OR dissolved inorganic carbon, not both. A bicarbonate concentration is not automatically a titrated alkalinity or total-carbon measurement. A reviewed bicarbonate-alkalinity result can be mapped as Alkalinity with basis HCO3. Unsupported/censored text such as <0.01 must be resolved explicitly.
  6. For redox-sensitive phases, select a measured/assumed pe or Eh (mV versus the standard hydrogen electrode, not an uncorrected reference-electrode reading). Eh is converted to pe using each sample's temperature. Total Fe and Fe(II) alone do not justify iron-oxide SI without an explicit redox assumption. Measured Fe(III) can constrain the ferric activity. Total Fe must not be added alongside separate Fe(II)/Fe(III) measurements.
  7. In explicit Calculate with PHREEQC mode, confirm the chemistry review checkbox and generate. Choose whether missing mapped measurements stop the run or exclude incomplete samples; exclusions keep the affected sample/mineral records in the audit tables as unassessed. Omitted elemental components are not imputed from other samples.
Mineral Components needed, in addition to pH and solution conditions
Calcite Ca and alkalinity or DIC
Dolomite Ca, Mg and alkalinity or DIC
Gypsum / Anhydrite Ca and sulfate
Halite Na and chloride
Quartz Dissolved silica
Fluorite Ca and fluoride
Goethite / Hematite Dissolved iron and a defensible ferric/redox constraint

Other phase names can be added. Verify their constituent chemistry and activity model in the chosen database. Even when a mineral's own components are measured, omitted major ions can bias ionic strength and SI. Database choice is especially important in saline waters.

Preserve the original laboratory reporting basis. PHREEQC converts volume-based concentrations to molality using density and the input formula masses; equivalent elemental/compound mass bases can produce small solvent-mass normalization differences at a fixed density.

Scientific definition and results#

PHREEQC calculates SI = log10(IAP / K(T)) from species activities and the temperature-dependent equilibrium constant of the chosen phase. Water activity participates where the database reaction requires it. Concentration products are never used as an SI approximation.

  • SI < 0: undersaturated; dissolution is thermodynamically possible if the mineral is present and accessible.
  • SI = 0: at equilibrium at the precision of the result.
  • SI > 0: supersaturated; precipitation is thermodynamically possible, but kinetics/nucleation may inhibit it.

No rounding or hidden equilibrium band changes these classes. SI is neither a reaction rate nor evidence of mineral presence. The engine speciates the initial solutions; it does not equilibrate them with minerals or adjust an ion to force charge balance.

Results & methods contains individual SI values and statuses; counts, mean, median, sample SD, minimum, maximum and quartiles by mineral; class counts/percentages; optional group summaries; scientific interpretations; and source provenance. Percentages exclude unassessed values and expose their count separately. SD is undefined for a single observation. Repeated/spatial observations are not assumed independent for inference.

Calculated results additionally include pH, pe, temperature, density, ionic strength, PHREEQC charge-balance percent error, log activities (including H2O), phase log K, reconstructed log IAP, exact PHREEQC input, full database text and engine/database hashes. In Results & methods → diagnostics, Charge balance error (%) shows each sample's signed numerical percentage at the returned precision. The final column, charge_balance_review, also shows Above 5% absolute error or Within 5% absolute error; exactly +5% or -5% is within the threshold. Excluded samples have a blank numeric value and an Unassessed review. Both columns are exported. An absolute charge-balance error above 5% remains a review warning, not a correction. Native-engine failure cancels the calculation without issuing partial SI results.

Interactive comparison and mapping#

Select minerals, samples and a group/season in Data. These selections stay shared when switching views; figure appearance remains specific to each plot. Use the application's Auto update, zoom, pan and style controls. Hover over plotted samples, bars and heatmap cells to inspect values. Scatter plots compare two minerals or SI against an actual groundwater measurement. Pair counts and exclusions are exported.

Heatmaps/maps have a diverging scale centred at zero, with an optional fixed symmetric range for comparisons. Grey heatmap cells are unassessed. Box/violin plots retain observed sample points; a violin density is not fabricated for fewer than three values or constant data. Bar comparisons allow up to 60 sample events / 300 sample-mineral combinations, and heatmaps up to 200 events; use filters for readable publication figures.

For GIS views, select distinct coordinate columns and explicitly declare projected/local distance coordinates or WGS84 longitude/latitude. The WGS84 distance approximation is restricted to study areas spanning no more than three degrees; use a suitable projected CRS for larger areas. Geographic basemaps require a declared CRS. Existing KML, KMZ and GeoJSON study boundaries, basemap attribution and map controls are reused.

IDW and ordinary kriging require at least three distinct, non-collinear complete locations and support at most 1,000 sites. Resolve co-located repeated observations by selecting an event/group or by deliberate aggregation outside this workflow. As in Maps & contours, an enabled study boundary is filled by default, including areas beyond the sample convex hull. Uncheck Fill entire boundary (extrapolate) to restrict predictions to the sampled area. Without a boundary, coverage uses the sample convex hull. Polygon holes and gaps between separate polygons remain unfilled. Extrapolated cells are flagged in the exported grid, and boundary vertices and source provenance accompany results. Saved explicit coverage preferences are preserved.

The Contour appearance card controls levels, contour lines and labels, line colour/width, fill opacity and the highlighted SI = 0 contour. Select ordinary kriging to enable Kriging prediction variance (SI²), which uses a separate nonnegative colour scale; its point overlays show observed sample locations, with original SI available on hover. Kriging range, partial sill and nugget are user supplied; no automatic variogram fit is implied. Predictions and variance are both retained in the grid table.

Spatial views share the Maps module's coordinate formats (decimal degrees, degrees/minutes or DMS), four-sided labels, grid design and spacing, true/grid north arrows, and draggable scale bars. Choose colour-scale visibility and right/bottom placement in Map layout. Sample colours can follow the SI scale or the fill colour in Style; point size, marker and opacity also apply. Basemaps use the provider and CRS in Data, with a contour overlay toggle and retry control. Map furniture positions and appearance are saved with the plot.

Interpolation acts on SI in logarithmic units. It does not speciate interpolated water chemistry, and is not evidence of a mineral boundary. Kriging variance is conditional on the variogram and excludes uncertainty in chemical measurements, redox and thermodynamic constants.

Exports and reproducibility#

Use the shared Export figure controls for high-resolution PNG (including 600 dpi), SVG or PDF. Set dimensions, labels, fonts and legend appearance in Style. Export Results & methods writes all tables to CSV or to an Excel workbook with Methods and Warnings sheets. Saved .aquaprov projects retain data, filters, mappings, custom databases, solution settings and per-plot styles. Generate again after reopening to reproduce the figures.

The packaged application contains only the Windows x64 native engine. On other platforms, imported SI analysis works normally; calculation requires setting AQUAPROV_IPHREEQC_LIBRARY to a compatible IPhreeqc shared library. Native work runs in AquaProv's cancellable child process.
