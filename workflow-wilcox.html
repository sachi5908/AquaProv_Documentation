Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

From laboratory concentrations to chemical equivalentsSodium percentage and digitized Wilcox curves

From laboratory concentrations to chemical equivalents

A mass concentration gives the mass of an ion; an equivalent concentration also accounts for its electric charge. Let Cᵢ be concentration in mg/L, Mᵢ molar mass in g/mol (numerically also mg/mmol), and |zᵢ| the absolute ionic charge (Hem, 1985):

mᵢ = Cᵢ / Mᵢ (mmol/L)
eᵢ = |zᵢ| Cᵢ / Mᵢ (meq/L)

For an input already in meq/L, eᵢ is the supplied value. To recover mmol/L from meq/L, divide by |zᵢ|. These conversions are different for divalent ions such as Ca²⁺ and monovalent ions such as Na⁺.

Ion Mᵢ (g/mol) Absolute charge
Ca 40.0780 2
Mg 24.3050 2
Na 22.9898 1
K 39.0983 1
HCO₃ 61.0171 1
CO₃ 60.0092 2
Cl 35.4527 1
SO₄ 96.0636 2

Illustrative check: 40.078 mg/L Ca is 1 mmol/L and 2 meq/L; 22.9898 mg/L Na is 1 mmol/L and 1 meq/L. Equal molar concentrations do not mean equal charge contributions. Concentrations must be finite and nonnegative. A missing measurement does not become a measured zero; check the selected missing-data policy and any explicitly supplied values.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/ions.py (ions_WEIGHT, ions_CHARGE), aquaprovpy/_chemistry.py (major_ion_meq).

Sodium percentage and digitized Wilcox curves

The Wilcox framework interprets irrigation-water composition using sodium percentage and electrical conductivity (Wilcox, 1948). The plotted percentage uses sodium alone in the numerator:

Na% = 100eNa / (eCa + eMg + eNa + eK)

All terms are meq/L. K remains in the denominator; the separately exported NaK_percent uses Na+K in the numerator and is not this ordinate. Illustrative check: Ca = 2, Mg = 1, Na = 3 and K = 1 meq/L give Na% = 42.857%, compared with NaK_percent = 57.143%. EC is measured at 25 °C in µS/cm.

Two historical curves, L(E) and U(E), are monotone piecewise cubic Hermite (PCHIP) interpolations of manually digitized points:

EC (µS/cm) 0 85 240 490 750 1000 1250 1500 1750 2000
L(E), Na% 100 91 81 71.5 65 60 56 53 50.5 48.5
U(E), Na% 100 98 95.5 91.5 88.5 85.5 83 80.5 78.5 76.5

The outer line is O(E) = 100 − 8E/3000 up to E = 3000. Apply the following rules in order, taking the first match: E < 750 and Na% < L → Excellent to good; E < 2000 and Na% < L → Good to permissible; E < 2000 and Na% < U → Permissible to doubtful; E < 3000 and Na% < O → Doubtful to unsuitable; otherwise Unsuitable. Exact boundaries enter the upper/right class. The reference domain is 0 < EC ≤ 3500 and 0 ≤ Na% ≤ 100.

Scan/digitization uncertainty remains. Points close to curves (1 percentage point) or relevant vertical boundaries (25 µS/cm) receive a review flag. These graphical classes do not establish suitability for a particular field.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/irrigation.py (calculate, wilcox_curves, classify_wilcox).

1. Import this worked input#

Module: Groundwater. Source: Sukinda_CGWB_Data_2024.xlsx / Selected_47_Samples. Prepared records: 47 (Sahoo et al., 2025).

All 47 rows; concentrations mg/L; measured EC in uS/cm. Historical graphical classifications.

Download this route’s CSV ↓
  1. Open Groundwater and choose Wilcox irrigation in the left Plot library.
  2. Choose Data → Import CSV or Excel and import the prepared CSV above. Confirm 47 rows. This CSV is a declared working copy; the original dataset is retained in Datasets.
  3. Keep Auto update off while entering settings. Check the Data panel below, including units, coordinates or measurement mappings when present.

4. Refine the Style panel#

Open Typography, Labels & ticks, Canvas & grid, Legend & color scale, or Dimensions & export. Use the illustrated Style reference for all five sections. Apply presentation changes, then inspect labels, legend and final physical dimensions. Sample grouping/symbol changes belong to Settings and require Generate.

5. Export and preserve#

  1. Choose a descriptive file name and an output folder you control. Use separate names for alternatives.
  2. Select PNG/TIFF for raster output or SVG/PDF for vector-compatible output. Set DPI for raster output; verify final dimensions.
  3. Click the named Export button. Save numerical Results & methods separately when available.
  4. Save the .aquaprov project.
