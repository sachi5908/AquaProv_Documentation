Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

From laboratory concentrations to chemical equivalents

A mass concentration gives the mass of an ion; an equivalent concentration also accounts for its electric charge. Let Cᵢ be concentration in mg/L, Mᵢ molar mass in g/mol (numerically also mg/mmol), and |zᵢ| the absolute ionic charge (Hem, 1985):

mᵢ = Cᵢ / Mᵢ (mmol/L)
eᵢ = |zᵢ| Cᵢ / Mᵢ (meq/L)

For an input already in meq/L, eᵢ is the supplied value. To recover mmol/L from meq/L, divide by |zᵢ|. These conversions are different for divalent ions such as Ca²⁺ and monovalent ions such as Na⁺.

Ion Mᵢ (g/mol) Absolute charge
Ca 40.0780 2
Mg 24.3050 2
Na 22.9898 1
K 39.0983 1
HCO₃ 61.0171 1
CO₃ 60.0092 2
Cl 35.4527 1
SO₄ 96.0636 2

Illustrative check: 40.078 mg/L Ca is 1 mmol/L and 2 meq/L; 22.9898 mg/L Na is 1 mmol/L and 1 meq/L. Equal molar concentrations do not mean equal charge contributions. Concentrations must be finite and nonnegative. A missing measurement does not become a measured zero; check the selected missing-data policy and any explicitly supplied values.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/ions.py (ions_WEIGHT, ions_CHARGE), aquaprovpy/_chemistry.py (major_ion_meq).

A polygon of equivalent concentrations

Stiff patterns use the converted equivalents directly, without normalizing them to percentages. The three paired levels connect Na+K with Cl, Ca with HCO₃, and Mg with SO₄ (Stiff, 1951):

Left magnitudes: eNa + eK, eCa, eMg
Right magnitudes: eCl, eHCO₃, eSO₄

Cations are drawn with negative horizontal coordinates only to place them left of the origin; the measured concentrations are nonnegative. Carbonate is not included in the bicarbonate vertex in this implementation. One polygon is generated per sample. If all six magnitudes double, the polygon doubles in horizontal width at a fixed axis scale. Its area is not a defined TDS, charge-balance or water-quality index.

Illustrative check: Na+K = 2, Ca = 3, Mg = 1 meq/L and Cl = 2, HCO₃ = 3, SO₄ = 1 meq/L produce equal opposing widths. Compare axis limits across figures before comparing polygon sizes.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/stiff.py (plot).

How it works#

A polygon joins cation and anion concentrations on paired horizontal axes. In this implementation Na + K, Ca, and Mg oppose Cl, HCO3, and SO4. One figure is generated for each sample.

Polygon width is expressed in meq/L.

Reference: Stiff (1951).

Generate in AquaProv#

  1. Open Groundwater & contamination and choose Stiff in the Plot library.
  2. Import your table and review the required columns. Use the preparation guide for normalized names and missing-data behavior.
  3. Set units, sample labels/groups, and appearance, then click Generate.
  4. Inspect the complete figure and any row-exclusion report before exporting.

Read the result#

Read axis values as well as shape. Compare figures at a consistent axis scale when judging relative magnitude. Use the figure selector to move through samples.

Interpretation limits#

Keep the measurement context

Unique, path-safe sample names are required; desktop generation is limited to 100 per-sample figures per run. Similar polygon shapes alone do not establish a shared source.
