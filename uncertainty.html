Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

Conditional Monte Carlo measurement propagationIndependent-row percentile bootstrap

Conditional Monte Carlo measurement propagation

Monte Carlo propagation evaluates an output model repeatedly using draws from specified input distributions (Joint Committee for Guides in Metrology, 2008). For a positive detected concentration with supplied mean m and SD s, AquaProv uses a moment-matched lognormal marginal. Laboratory mode uses mapped SDs, scenario mode uses s = r·m for the selected relative SD r, and fixed mode uses a constant:

σ²log = ln[1 + (s/m)²]
μlog = ln(m) − σ²log/2
C* = exp(μlog + σlogZ),   Z ∼ Normal(0,1)

An optional shared log-error correlation ρ uses Zᵢ = √ρ Z₀ + √(1−ρ)εᵢ, with independent standard normal Z₀ and εᵢ. This is a specified log-error scenario, not an estimated concentration correlation. Zero with positive SD has no supported nonnegative moment-matched distribution; a missing laboratory SD is unassessed in laboratory mode.

Each draw is converted to equivalents and passed through the same SAR, sodium-percentage and graphical classification formulas as the deterministic diagrams. Reported intervals are the finite-draw 2.5th, 50th and 97.5th percentiles. Class and threshold probabilities are indicator frequencies:

P̂(event) = (1/B)∑b=1…B 1(event in draw b)
Monte Carlo SE = √[P̂(1−P̂)/B]

Wilson intervals describe binomial sampling precision for an estimated probability (Wilson, 1927). The exported 95% Wilson bounds and split-half difference describe simulation precision. They do not quantify whether the chosen SDs, distributions or correlation are scientifically correct. The irrigation interval view plots SAR and Na% intervals by sample; class probabilities are in the result tables. All conclusions are conditional on the chosen measurement and censoring model.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/uncertainty.py (draw_observation, draw_group, interval, probability, propagate).

Independent-row percentile bootstrap

For n retained measurements, AquaProv draws B replacement samples of size n. It computes the selected mean or median θ*b from each sample. The observed estimate θ̂ is calculated on the original retained data (Efron & Tibshirani, 1993):

Confidence interval = [Qα/2(θ*), Q1−α/2(θ*)]
α = 1 − confidence/100
Bootstrap SE = sample SD of θ*₁,…,θ*B

The histogram contains the B statistic estimates, not the original measurements. At 95% confidence the interval uses the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles (NumPy linear quantiles). Defaults are 2,000 resamples and seed 42; the accepted range is 200–20,000 with at least three complete observations. The seed makes a given configuration reproducible, not scientifically certain.

The interval concerns sampling variation of a mean or median under independent, representative rows. It is neither a prediction interval for a new measurement nor propagation of laboratory SD. Repeated observations at wells require an appropriate grouped design such as the well-bootstrap assessment. Constant observations produce a degenerate interval; small samples may give poor coverage.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/exploration.py (bootstrap, plot).

Choose an uncertainty mode#

ModeBehaviorInterpretation
FixedDetected measurements remain fixed.No measurement-error distribution is inferred.
Relative-SD scenarioPositive observations use lognormal draws with an explicitly chosen relative SD.Sensitivity to an assumed error level, not laboratory-calibrated uncertainty.
Laboratory SDPositive observations use supplied SDs to match lognormal mean and variance.Missing SDs prevent those detected observations from being propagated.

Choose 200–20,000 Monte Carlo draws and a nonnegative seed. Shared log-error correlation in [0, 1) is an optional explicit covariance scenario; zero means independent positive-observation errors. Censoring draws remain independent. A mean of zero with a positive SD cannot have a nonnegative distribution with that mean and is not propagated.

Censoring alternatives#

ChoiceBehavior
ExcludeDo not propagate the censored measurement.
UniformDraw uniformly from zero to the declared reporting limit.
Half limitUse a fixed value equal to half the declared limit.

Uniform and half-limit treatments are assumptions, not recovered measurements. Scenario comparison displays alternatives separately without averaging across them or assigning probabilities to the choice of model. Method-selection context: Pianosi et al. (2016).

A worked scenario#

Metric intervals are marginal conditional 95% intervals. Piper coordinate intervals are not joint confidence regions; dominant-ion facies use a >50% criterion. Monte Carlo class frequencies retain the historical USSL/Wilcox boundaries. Wilson probability bounds describe finite-simulation precision, not confidence in the scientific assumptions.

Selected chemical screening profile#

The implemented default profile is WHO-2022-selected-chemicals-v1, based on World Health Organization (2022). It includes only the following named species:

AnalyteUpper screening valueBasis
NO350 mg/LNitrate species, not nitrate as N
F1.5 mg/LFluoride
Cr_total0.05 mg/LTotal chromium
Ni0.07 mg/LNickel
A limited chemical screen

This is not a complete drinking-water compliance assessment. Unmapped or unassessable chemicals must be reported as such. A low probability for these four analytes does not establish that water is safe to drink. Verify the standard and jurisdiction required for the actual study.

Follow-up assay priorities#

Priority score = min(p, 1 − p) / assay costp is the conditional threshold exceedance probability. Costs are equal in this implemented ranking.

The method ranks threshold ambiguity and applies a budget. It exports baseline ranks based on distance to threshold and exceedance probability. Unassessable observations cannot receive a probability-based priority. This is a review heuristic, not a validated value-of-information calculation, health-risk ranking, or optimal monitoring design.

Report the assumptions#

  • Measurement species, units, basis, detection limits, qualifiers, and laboratory SD provenance.
  • Mode, relative SD, error correlation, censoring choice, draw count, seed, and threshold-profile identifier.
  • Unassessable observations and outputs, graphical boundary limitations, and sensitivity across alternatives.
  • Simulation precision separately from uncertainty in the input distributions or sampling design.
