Mathematics and scientific background #

Follow the calculation from inputs to plotted values. The equations and conventions below follow the AquaProv Python implementation. Read them alongside this guide’s worked data, settings and exported results.

Charge balance and measurement-status counts

Charge-balance error compares measured charge totals, without forcing them to agree (Hem, 1985):

CBE (%) = 100(Tc − Ta)/(Tc + Ta)

Both totals use meq/L and all eight core ions must be measured. Positive CBE means excess measured cation charge; negative means excess measured anion charge. The default review flag is |CBE| > 5%; the user can set the tolerance. For Tc = 5 and Ta = 4 meq/L, CBE = 11.111%. A small CBE can result from offsetting analytical errors and is not proof of accuracy.

The assessment also exports a separate CBE including nitrate and fluoride when both are measured; it does not silently add these to the core-ion diagnostic. Missing CO₃ is not assumed zero. The status bar chart counts analyte observations (cells) by detected, zero, missing, censored or other parsed status, rather than counting only sample rows. Inspect species/reporting-basis conversion and exclusions before interpreting downstream plots.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/measurements.py (prepare, quality_tables), aquaprovpy/assessment/engine.py.

Conditional Monte Carlo measurement propagation

Monte Carlo propagation evaluates an output model repeatedly using draws from specified input distributions (Joint Committee for Guides in Metrology, 2008). For a positive detected concentration with supplied mean m and SD s, AquaProv uses a moment-matched lognormal marginal. Laboratory mode uses mapped SDs, scenario mode uses s = r·m for the selected relative SD r, and fixed mode uses a constant:

σ²log = ln[1 + (s/m)²]
μlog = ln(m) − σ²log/2
C* = exp(μlog + σlogZ),   Z ∼ Normal(0,1)

An optional shared log-error correlation ρ uses Zᵢ = √ρ Z₀ + √(1−ρ)εᵢ, with independent standard normal Z₀ and εᵢ. This is a specified log-error scenario, not an estimated concentration correlation. Zero with positive SD has no supported nonnegative moment-matched distribution; a missing laboratory SD is unassessed in laboratory mode.

Each draw is converted to equivalents and passed through the same SAR, sodium-percentage and graphical classification formulas as the deterministic diagrams. Reported intervals are the finite-draw 2.5th, 50th and 97.5th percentiles. Class and threshold probabilities are indicator frequencies:

P̂(event) = (1/B)∑b=1…B 1(event in draw b)
Monte Carlo SE = √[P̂(1−P̂)/B]

Wilson intervals describe binomial sampling precision for an estimated probability (Wilson, 1927). The exported 95% Wilson bounds and split-half difference describe simulation precision. They do not quantify whether the chosen SDs, distributions or correlation are scientifically correct. The irrigation interval view plots SAR and Na% intervals by sample; class probabilities are in the result tables. All conclusions are conditional on the chosen measurement and censoring model.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/uncertainty.py (draw_observation, draw_group, interval, probability, propagate).

Conditional probability of an upper-threshold exceedance

Chemical guideline values provide substance-specific comparison levels that require interpretation in their health and exposure context (World Health Organization, 2026). For selected species concentration C and a named threshold H in mg/L of that same species, the event is strictly C > H. Its probability is the fraction of simulation draws above H. A draw exactly equal to H is not an exceedance. For example, 120 exceedances among 2,000 draws give P̂ = 0.06 and Monte Carlo SE ≈ 0.00531.

The screening figure shows up to 30 assessable assay probabilities in its left panel; its right histogram uses all assessable probabilities with bins from 0 to 1. No supported distribution means unassessed, not P = 0. The default versioned assessment profile is WHO-2022-selected-chemicals-v1 (NO₃ 50, F 1.5, total Cr 0.05 and Ni 0.07 mg/L); it is distinct from the configurable WQI standards profiles. Review the actual exported profile and reporting basis.

For censored observations, logical bounds can sometimes be reported without a distribution: C < L with L ≤ H implies no exceedance under that reporting inequality; C > L with L > H implies exceedance. Otherwise the probability may remain anywhere in [0,1]. Those bounds assume a valid reporting limit. Screening a few chemical species does not establish overall drinking-water safety.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/uncertainty.py (WHO_SCREENING, propagate), aquaprovpy/assessment/engine.py.

Follow-up assay priorities and the budget rule

The default ambiguity priority is largest near P = 0.5 and zero at P = 0 or 1:

Ambiguity = min(P, 1 − P)
Priority score = ambiguity / assay cost
Budget eligibility: rank × equal assay cost ≤ budget, with priority score > 0

Candidates are sorted by descending score, then ascending relative threshold margin |median−H|/H, source row and analyte. The left panel displays exceedance probabilities in this priority order; the right displays scores, with budget-eligible assays highlighted. High exceedance probability and high uncertainty are different reasons to act. This default score is a review heuristic, not a health-risk or value-of-information calculation.

The optional EVSI method uses a normal current log concentration X ∼ N(μ,σ²) and an independent follow-up log assay Y|X ∼ N(X,τ²). Its posterior has gain k = σ²/(σ²+τ²), mean μ′ = μ+k(Y−μ), and variance σ′² = (1−k)σ². With unit loss for an incorrect binary threshold decision:

Current risk = min(P, 1−P)
EVSI = current risk − EY[min(P′, 1−P′)]
EVSI priority = EVSI / assay cost

The expectation uses numerical Gaussian integration in expected_value. Equal assay costs, stable chemistry, additive binary loss and no information sharing across assays are assumed. Unsupported censored current distributions cannot receive this EVSI rank. Inspect excluded assays as well as the ranked list.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/uncertainty.py (propagate), aquaprovpy/assessment/decision.py (posterior_log_parameters, expected_value).

Sensitivity to uncertainty and censoring assumptions

Sensitivity analysis examines how assumptions and input choices affect model conclusions (Pianosi et al., 2016). The comparison runs four alternatives: fixed detected values with censored cells excluded; relative-SD draws with censored cells excluded; relative-SD draws with C ∼ Uniform(0,L) for left-censored cells; and relative-SD draws with fixed C = L/2. Uniform censoring has mean L/2 and variance L²/12, whereas half-limit substitution has no within-limit variability.

Scenario probability range = [minsP̂s(C>H), maxsP̂s(C>H)]

The range plot shows up to 40 assessable assays and the bar chart summarizes conclusion labels across scenarios. “Stable below” requires all four scenarios to be assessable with every P ≤ 0.05; “stable above” requires every P ≥ 0.95. Other fully assessed cases are scenario-dependent or ambiguous, and incomplete cases have insufficient information. These are display rules, not regulatory thresholds. The span is not a probability-weighted average or a confidence interval: the software assigns no probabilities to the assumptions themselves. For a separate propagation run, a supplied lognormal model truncated below L is also supported, but it is not one of these four comparison cases.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/uncertainty.py (compare_scenarios, draw_observation), aquaprovpy/assessment/censoring.py.

Compositional log-ratio PCA

For D positive, non-overlapping concentration components xᵢ, the geometric mean g(x) removes the arbitrary overall concentration scale. AquaProv computes centered log-ratios and projects them into an orthonormal Helmert basis B of the zero-sum subspace (Aitchison, 1982; Egozcue et al., 2003):

g(x) = exp[(1/D)∑ⱼln xⱼ]
clrⱼ(x) = ln[xⱼ/g(x)]
ilr(x) = clr(x) B;   BᵀB = I,   Bᵀ1 = 0
dA(x,y) = ‖ilr(x) − ilr(y)‖₂

The D−1 ILR coordinates are centered, not standardized, before SVD PCA. Sample scores use U DSVD, and eigenvalues are dₖ²/(n−1). The exported clr_loadings are B V, directions in log-ratio space; they are not the variance-scaled loadings from the ordinary PCA module. Ward clustering uses Euclidean distance on ILR coordinates (Aitchison distance). The left plot shows PC1–PC2 scores coloured by Ward cluster, and the compositional variance panel shows component percentages.

At least three parts and four strictly positive complete samples are needed. Zero, missing and censored components are excluded with an audit; no pseudocount is added. pH, EC, TDS and alkalinity are not compositional parts here, and total Cr must not overlap with Cr(VI). Multiplying a sample by any positive constant leaves its log ratios unchanged; selected components determine the subcomposition and interpretation.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/robustness.py (compositional).

Bootstrap cluster co-assignment

Bootstrap resampling probes sensitivity to the observed sample, while Ward clustering defines groups by a squared-dispersion objective (Efron & Tibshirani, 1993; Ward, 1963). For each of B row-bootstrap resamples, AquaProv fits Ward clusters to the resampled ILR observations, calculates their centroids, and assigns all original rows to the nearest resampled centroid. The consensus matrix is:

Aᵢⱼ = (1/B)∑b 1[assigned_clusterb(i) = assigned_clusterb(j)]

It is symmetric, lies in [0,1], and has diagonal 1. A value of 0.9 means the pair was assigned together in 90% of bootstrap runs. The heatmap orders rows by their original cluster for readability; the exported matrix preserves identifiers. Per-resample adjusted Rand indices compare the whole resampled partition with the original, correcting agreement for chance.

These frequencies depend on the fixed variable selection, cluster count and nearest-centroid assignment rule. They are not probabilities that a hydrogeological grouping is true. Row resampling assumes independent samples; the implementation rejects repeated well identifiers for this stability route. Use one scientifically justified campaign per well or an appropriate grouped inference design.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/robustness.py (compositional, resampling branch).

Training-only variograms and spatial block validation

Spatially structured validation separates training and evaluation observations to reduce optimism from spatial dependence (Roberts et al., 2017). Assessment creates 3–10 geographic K-means blocks using coordinates only. Each block is held out in turn, and its variogram is fitted using the remaining observations. The empirical Matheron semivariance in a distance bin is:

γ̂(h) = [1/(2Nh)]∑(i,j) in bin h(vᵢ − vⱼ)²

Ten equal-width bins extend to 0.8 times the maximum training separation, with at least three pairs per retained bin and at least four populated bins for fitting. Bounded least squares fits γ(h) = s+n₀−C(h), weighting each residual by √(Nh/∑Nh) and scaling it by the training sample variance. Range, sill and nugget are refitted for every fold. The full-data variogram shown beside held-out predictions is a separate diagnostic; it is never used for those holdouts.

For held-out errors eᵢ = predictionᵢ − observationᵢ:

RMSE = √[(1/n)∑ᵢeᵢ²];   MAE = (1/n)∑ᵢ|eᵢ|;   bias = (1/n)∑ᵢeᵢ
Conditional 95% interval = prediction ± 1.96√variance

The exported residual column uses the opposite sign (observed − prediction); exported bias follows the equation above. IDW (power 2) and the training mean provide baselines. Coverage is the fraction of holdouts in their intervals. With a selected threshold, Brier score is mean squared difference between predicted exceedance probability and the observed exceedance indicator.

The plot compares observed and held-out predicted values with a 1:1 reference line. It requires at least max(15, 3 × blocks) observations; individual training fits need at least eight varying observations. Blocks have no separation buffer. Fits reaching parameter bounds, cross-boundary dependence and Gaussian intervals below zero need scientific review.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/spatial.py (fit_variogram, spatial_validation).

Fitted prediction map and conditional uncertainty

After spatial block validation, this route fits the variogram to the full retained data and applies ordinary kriging to a hull-masked grid. The left panel displays predictions. The right displays conditional SD = √variance unless an upper threshold H is configured, in which case it displays (Cressie, 1993):

P(v > H | fitted model) = 1 − Φ[(H − v̂)/σpred]

Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution. If SD is zero, the result is the indicator v̂ > H. These model-based probabilities differ from the measurement Monte Carlo probabilities elsewhere in Assessment. They assume Gaussian prediction errors and omit fitted-parameter uncertainty. Read holdout RMSE, coverage and parameter-bound flags before interpreting the map; smoother or denser grids are not additional validation.

The updated gallery export evaluates the same full-data fitted variogram across the entire supplied KML on a 120 × 120 grid. Both the prediction and conditional SD fields reach the boundary, with extrapolated cells flagged in the download. Training-only fits and held-out validation scores remain unchanged. This export uses geographic labels, true north and geodesic scales.

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/engine.py (view='spatial_map'), aquaprovpy/assessment/spatial.py.

Bootstrap wells as sampling units

Within each well j, calculate the mean mⱼ of its available measured values. The target is the equally weighted mean over J wells:

θ̂well = (1/J)∑ⱼmⱼ
θ̂pooled = ∑ⱼnⱼmⱼ / ∑ⱼnⱼ

The two estimates differ when wells have unequal sampling counts. The bootstrap resamples J whole-well means with replacement and averages each draw; its 95% interval is the 2.5th–97.5th percentile range. At least three distinct wells are required. This addresses between-well sampling variation, conditional on the observed well summaries; it does not estimate laboratory error or remove spatial dependence among wells (Efron & Tibshirani, 1993).

If time is mapped, repeated measurements at each identical time are first averaged within a well. With at least three distinct times, the descriptive Theil–Sen slope is median[(vⱼ−vᵢ)/(tⱼ−tᵢ)] over unequal time pairs; Kendall's tau describes monotonic association. ISO dates give slope units per 365.25-day year, while numeric time retains its source unit. No serial-independence significance tests or forecasts are added. The left panel is a histogram of well means with the equal-well estimate and its bootstrap interval. The right shows the distribution of available slopes, or measurement counts per well when slopes are unavailable. Full counts, trends and bootstrap summaries are exported (Sen, 1968).

Implementation: aquaprovpy/assessment/robustness.py (well_inference), aquaprovpy/assessment/engine.py.

Start with the measurement model#

Open Assessment & uncertainty and inspect the suggested schema. Map analytes and metadata, then confirm source units and chemical basis. Supported concentration units include mg/L, µg/L, meq/L, and mmol/L where chemically meaningful; pH and EC have their own units. The supplied trace-metal ppb headers are suggested as µg/L and must be verified.

Preserve detection qualifiers, limits, and standard deviations as explicit fields. Alkalinity as CaCO3 is not treated as HCO3. Total chromium and hexavalent chromium are separate analytes; do not use a total-Cr threshold as if it were specific to Cr(VI).

Charge balance#

CBE (%) = 100 × (Σcations − Σanions) / (Σcations + Σanions)Both sums use equivalent concentrations from the eight major ions.

The default absolute-CBE review tolerance is 5%. A complete balance requires all eight mapped major-ion measurements, including carbonate; a missing ion is not silently assumed zero. This major-ion balance does not include nitrate and trace species. A separate CBE_with_NO3_F_percent diagnostic is exported when both nitrate and fluoride are measured; it adds those two anions explicitly.

Duplicate records or matching chemistry are review flags, not proof that two rows are laboratory replicates. Audit flags do not automatically filter the other modules.

Compositional analysis#

Select at least three positive, non-overlapping concentration components and at least four usable rows. pH, EC, TDS, and alkalinity are not compositional parts in this workflow. Total Cr and Cr(VI) cannot both be included. Missing, censored, zero, or negative parts are excluded explicitly; no pseudocount is added.

The implementation forms centered log ratios and projects them into an orthonormal Helmert basis. ILR balances are centered, not standardized, before PCA. The exported basis, center, variance, and CLR loading contrasts define the model. Component selection changes the subcomposition being studied.

Cluster stability resamples independent rows, fits Ward groups, then assigns all rows to nearest training centroids. Co-assignment frequencies and adjusted Rand indices describe this procedure’s stability, not the probability that groups are real aquifers. Repeated-well rows must be reduced using a justified selection first. Hydrochemical CoDA context: Owen et al. (2016).

Spatial validation#

Map X/Y and select a measurement. Use 3–10 geographic blocks and at least max(15, 3 × blocks) complete observations. Coordinate-only K-means defines blocks. Each held-out block is predicted using a variogram fitted solely on its training observations; IDW and the training mean are comparison baselines.

Exports include RMSE, MAE, signed bias, 95% interval coverage, interval width, held-out predictions, and variogram parameters. A threshold also enables conditional exceedance probabilities and a Brier score. Fitted parameter bounds and weakly identified models are reported.

The full-data variogram is a separate diagnostic and fitted-map input. The map uses a 70 × 70 grid masked to the sample convex hull. Conditional Gaussian intervals omit parameter uncertainty, can extend below zero, and are not clipped. Blocks have no buffer, so short-range dependence across block edges can remain.

Repeated wells#

Map well identity and, for descriptive trends, time. At least three distinct wells are needed. Well inference averages each well’s measurements and resamples whole well summaries, weighting wells equally. It exports both the equal-well mean and the pooled measurement mean; these can differ when wells have unequal sampling counts.

Within-well descriptive trends use means at each distinct time, Theil–Sen slopes, and Kendall association. At least three distinct times are required. Numeric years retain their source time unit; ISO dates use years of 365.25 days. No serial-independence p-values or forecasts are claimed. Resampling wells does not itself remove spatial dependence.
