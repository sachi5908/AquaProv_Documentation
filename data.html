Prepare your data

Preserve the meaning of measurements before choosing a diagram or statistical method.

Table structure#

Use a header row and one record per sample or sampling event. Import CSV, XLS, or XLSX; choose the intended worksheet explicitly. Do not combine units within a column. Keep laboratory identifiers as text when leading zeros matter. In Excel, display-only number formatting does not change the stored identifier.

Headers and units#

Chemical concentrations must be interpreted with their units, species and charge basis (Hem, 1985). Common laboratory headers are normalized on import. The original names remain in provenance, but a recognized name does not prove that units are correct. If two source columns normalize to the same name, import stops: keep one verified version of each chemical species.

meq/L = (mg/L × |z|) / Mz is ionic charge; M is molecular weight in g/mol. Weights are taken from aquaprovpy/ions.py.

1 mS/cm = 1 dS/m = 1,000 µS/cm. A bare EC header defaults to µS/cm in the interface; verify it against laboratory metadata. In Assessment, explicitly select species, units, and basis. Nitrate reported as N and nitrate reported as NO3 require different conversions.

Missing, zero, and censored values#

InputMeaningTreatment
BlankMissing observationReview complete-case exclusions or choose to stop. A blank is not zero.
0Reported numeric zeroKeep its meaning; log axes and log-ratio methods cannot represent zero directly.
<0.01Left-censored resultUse a declared detection limit and censoring treatment in Assessment.
n.d. / NDNon-detect or ambiguous qualifierRecover the reporting limit and laboratory definition; no numeric limit can be invented.
Negative concentrationInvalid for major-ion compositionReview the source; normalized diagrams reject negative concentrations.
Two different missing-data workflows

Ordinary plots use complete required records and report exclusions. The statistical engine rejects nonnumeric qualifiers; Assessment provides explicit qualifier, limit, uncertainty, and censoring controls. Neither route silently turns missing ions into zeros.

Select variables deliberately#

Log-ratio analysis treats relative composition through ratios rather than independent raw percentages (Aitchison, 1982; Egozcue et al., 2003). For statistics, review IDs, dates, coordinate columns, constant variables, and redundant totals before including them. Do not analyze Ca, Mg, and hardness as independent chemical species without considering their relationship. Do not include both Na and a Na+K total as if they were unrelated components.

Major-ion proportions sum to a fixed total. Correlation and PCA on such compositions can behave differently from analysis of absolute concentrations. Use compositional PCA when log-ratio analysis matches your question.

Coordinates and repeated records#

The Yang worked data are attributed to their associated source publication (Yang et al., 2020). The Liu worked data are attributed to their associated source publication (Liu et al., 2021). Use decimal degrees for WGS84 longitude/latitude. Longitude is X; latitude is Y. Verify zone, hemisphere, units, and datum for projected data. The Liu and Yang files include repeated observations, so a row count is not an independent-site count. Coincident sites must be resolved deliberately before interpolation; do not silently average across different sampling dates.

For dataset-specific caveats, open the bundled dataset catalog.
